Don't Build Your Own Bootloader

Why you should skip the bootloader and focus on the kernel.

December 1, 2025

Journey §

I have been building my x64 playground OS, mangoOS, on and off for the past year. My goal was to understand everything about operating systems, from the boot process to user space.

I started by reading the OSDev wiki on rolling your own bootloader, and it was quite a ride. I spent weeks trying to figure out why my string literals weren't working, only to discover I was just ONE sector off from reading the kernel even though everything else loaded correctly.

Call this what this is—skill issue. 🫠

Lessons Learned §

Well, you'll encounter issues like these when working on an OS. But this bootloader is far from being complete. What about file system support? Support for ELF? 🤔 You can spend all your time just working on the bootloader. That's where it hit me: is this where all my effort should go?

In the end, I did get a somewhat working bootloader (ignisOS-bootloader), but on the kernel side I only have a basic "Hello World."

Recommendation §

Pro tip: skip the bootloader and focus on the kernel!

Use an existing bootloader like GRUB, Limine, or another option and move quickly. A boot loader is not part of the OS. It's simply a tool to get the system into a state where the kernel can run, and it can be a major distraction. Only create a custom bootloader if you truly need capabilities that existing solutions don't provide.
